Double Concentrations : Students concentrating in Critical Native American and Indigenous Studies and another discipline may count no more than two courses towards both concentrations.
Transfer credits : Credits for courses taken at other institutions may be applied to the Critical NAIS concentration following review by the NAIS DUS. No more than four (4) transfer courses can be applied toward this A.B.

---------------
Language Study:
---------------
Through a focus on the Language and Identity Breadth Area, students may include one year (two courses) of language study (determined by the student and DUS, which could be met through a DISP or a GISP course). The language may be an Indigenous one but does not need to be; rather, it should align closely with the goals and interests of the concentrator and could contribute to research and learning goals through access to records, manuscripts and other documents not in English. Students not focusing on Language and Identity may also be interested in language study and are encouraged to include that in their Concentration if it contributes to their goals and objectives.

------------------------------------
Honors Thesis or Project (Optional):
------------------------------------
Students may choose to complete their Critical NAIS concentration with honors by completing an honors thesis. The form of the thesis should be determined in consultation with the student’s faculty advisor(s), and can be composed of formal academic writing, community engaged work, a creative project, or blend these methods. In addition to other course requirements and the 10 credits needed to complete the concentration, to successfully complete Honors in the Critical NAIS concentration, a student must:
  • outline a viable project and determine its form (e.g. academic writing, community engaged research, a creative project, or a hybrid form)
  • have the support of at least one faculty advisor
  • receive approval for a thesis proposal submitted to a faculty advisor and the CNAIS DUS

-------------------
Thesis Requirements
-------------------
  • Good academic standing at the University and in the concentration (see below)
  • Completion of a thesis based on extended independent research under the guidance of a Brown University faculty advisor(s). Community or non-Brown advisors may be part of an advising team, chaired by a Brown faculty member.
  • Enrollment in two thesis preparation courses (with thesis advisor) over the final year of undergraduate study (in the fall and spring semesters of final year)
  • Submission of an Honors Thesis prospectus and application for honors designation within the Critical NAIS concentration (to the CNAIS DUS and faculty advisor(s)) at the end of the third year of study or by Sept. 30 of the final year. The prospectus should be a three to five page (double spaced) project description outlining method, the form of thesis, and include a bibliography of relevant sources (this does not count toward the page count of the project description). Completed thesis submitted to the NAISI DUS and faculty advisor no later than the second  week of April (for May graduation) or the first Monday of December (for winter graduation).

----------------------
Academic Requirements:
----------------------
  • At least two consecutive semesters in good academic standing at the time of application (submission of honors prospectus)
  • The honors thesis must earn an A (or a recommendation for Honors) from two faculty readers (the thesis advisor and one additional reader), in addition to fulfilling all other concentration requirements.
If a student completes an honors thesis that is not conferred honors status, the two semesters of preparation will receive the letter grades given by the faculty advisor of record for the thesis courses. The independent studies do not count toward the required 1000-level courses.
